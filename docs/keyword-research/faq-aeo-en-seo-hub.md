## H1 — SEO pillar → `/en/blog/what-is-seo`

**What is SEO and how does it work?**  
SEO (Search Engine Optimization) is the practice of improving a website so it ranks higher in unpaid Google results. Google crawls pages, indexes them, then ranks them by relevance, quality, and trust. On-page content, technical health, and authoritative links are the main levers.

**What does SEO stand for?**  
SEO stands for Search Engine Optimization — the process of making your site more visible in organic search for the queries your buyers actually type.

**How do I start SEO for beginners?**  
Start with clear titles and meta descriptions, one focused page per important query, a fast mobile site, and Google Business Profile if you serve a local market. Then add consistent content and measure progress in Search Console.

**Is SEO dead or evolving in 2026?**  
SEO is evolving, not dead. Classic rankings still drive traffic, while AI Overviews and ChatGPT-style answers reward clear definitions, FAQs, and citable expertise. Brands that optimize for both Google and AI visibility win.

**Can ChatGPT do SEO?**  
ChatGPT can help brainstorm topics, outlines, and FAQs, but it cannot replace crawl fixes, real keyword demand data, or earning links. Use AI as an assistant inside a measured SEO process — not as the whole strategy.

**How to improve SEO?**  
Fix technical blockers first, align each money page to one primary intent, expand helpful content, strengthen internal links, and earn relevant backlinks. Track impressions and clicks in Search Console weekly.

**Why is SEO important?**  
SEO captures high-intent demand without paying per click. It compounds: pages that rank keep bringing leads while ads stop when budget stops. For most businesses it is the most durable acquisition channel.

**What are the 4 types of SEO?**  
The four common types are technical SEO, on-page SEO, off-page (including link building), and local SEO. Content SEO and AI/GEO visibility often sit across these pillars in 2026 programs.

**How long does SEO take to work?**  
Expect early movement in 2–3 months and meaningful growth in 4–12 months, depending on competition, site history, and publishing cadence. SEO is cumulative — consistency beats one-off campaigns.

**SEO vs SEM — what's the difference?**  
SEO is organic (unpaid) visibility. SEM usually means paid search ads. Most growth plans use both: ads for immediate demand, SEO for long-term cost efficiency.

## H2 — Keyword research → `/en/tools/keyword-research`, `/en/blog/keyword-research-guide`

**How do you do keyword research for SEO?**  
Start from seed topics your buyers care about, expand with related terms and questions, group by search intent, then prioritize by volume, difficulty, and business value. Map one primary keyword (and close variants) to each page.

**What are SEO keywords?**  
SEO keywords are the words and phrases people type into search engines when looking for information, products, or services. Good keyword research matches those phrases to pages that can convert.

**What are the 4 types of keywords for SEO?**  
Common types: informational (learn), navigational (find a brand), commercial (compare), and transactional (buy). Local modifiers (“near me”, city names) often cut across commercial and transactional intent.

**How to find keywords for SEO?**  
Use Search Console queries you already impress for, autocomplete/PAA questions, competitor pages, and a keyword tool for volume and difficulty. Prioritize phrases you can serve with unique, useful pages.

**What is keyword difficulty?**  
Keyword difficulty estimates how hard it is to rank in the top 10 organic results, usually on a 0–100 scale. Lower KD with clear commercial intent is often the best place to start for newer sites.

## H3 — SEO tools → `/en/tools`, `/en/blog/best-seo-tools-2026`

**What are the SEO tools?**  
SEO tools help you research keywords, audit technical issues, track rankings, analyze backlinks, and measure Search Console performance. Free checkers cover basics; paid suites scale research and monitoring.

**Which SEO tool is best for beginners?**  
Beginners should start with Google Search Console plus one free audit/checker and a simple keyword tool. Add a paid suite only when you need larger keyword databases or link data weekly.

**Free vs paid SEO tools?**  
Free tools are enough for audits, meta tags, and basic research. Paid tools win on depth of keyword indexes, historical backlinks, and competitor coverage — useful once you publish consistently.

## H4 — SEO services → `/en/services/seo-audits` (+ related services)

**What are SEO services?**  
SEO services are done-for-you (or done-with-you) work to improve organic visibility: audits, keyword strategy, on-page optimization, technical fixes, content, local SEO, and link building — usually delivered as a monthly package with reporting.

**How much does SEO cost?**  
Serious monthly SEO for small businesses often starts around a few hundred euros and scales with competition, site size, and content needs. Ask for a scoped package with deliverables — not vague “ranking guarantees.”

**What's included in professional SEO services?**  
Typical packages include technical audit, keyword mapping, on-page fixes, content briefs or publishing, internal linking, and monthly reporting. Local or e-commerce programs add GBP, citations, or category/product SEO.

**Do small businesses need SEO services?**  
Yes if customers search for what you sell. Small businesses often win faster on local and long-tail terms than on national head terms — especially with Google Business Profile and focused service pages.

**What are AI SEO services?**  
AI SEO (GEO/AEO) helps your brand get cited in AI answers — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews — through clear entities, FAQ schema, and citation-ready content alongside classic rankings.

## H5 — Local SEO → `/en/services/local-seo`, `/en/blog/local-seo-guide`

**What is local SEO?**  
Local SEO is the practice of ranking in map packs and local organic results for “near me” and city queries. It centers on Google Business Profile, NAP consistency, reviews, local pages, and citations.

**Does local SEO still work?**  
Yes. Map pack and “near me” searches remain high-intent. Businesses with complete profiles, strong reviews, and unique location pages still win customers over generic national sites.

**How to do local SEO for beginners?**  
Claim and fully complete Google Business Profile, match NAP everywhere, collect recent reviews, build one strong location/service page, and track calls/directions. Then expand to more neighborhoods or cities.

**How much does local SEO cost?**  
Local SEO retainers vary by market competition and number of locations. Many small businesses start with a focused GBP + citations + location pages package, then scale monthly content and reviews.

## H6 — SEO audit → `/en/tools/free-seo-audit`, `/en/services/seo-audits`

**What is an SEO audit?**  
An SEO audit is a structured review of technical health, indexation, on-page relevance, content gaps, and authority signals. It prioritizes fixes that unlock rankings fastest instead of dumping a 200-item spreadsheet.

**How often should you do an SEO audit?**  
Run a full audit when launching a new site, after a redesign, or every 6–12 months. Spot-check Core Web Vitals, index coverage, and top money pages monthly via Search Console.

**Free vs paid SEO audit?**  
Free checkers catch common issues quickly. A paid/professional audit adds prioritization, competitor context, and a roadmap tied to business goals — then implementation, which free tools never do alone.

## H7 — Technical SEO → `/en/blog/technical-seo-guide`

**What is technical SEO?**  
Technical SEO makes your site easy to crawl, index, and render: speed, mobile usability, HTTPS, sitemaps, canonicals, structured data, and clean architecture. It is the foundation under content and links.

**Technical SEO vs on-page SEO?**  
Technical SEO is how the site works for crawlers and performance. On-page SEO is how each URL’s content, titles, and structure match search intent. You need both for durable rankings.

**Do I need a technical SEO audit?**  
Yes if rankings stall, pages aren’t indexed, Core Web Vitals fail, or you shipped a redesign. A technical audit finds blockers that content alone cannot fix.

## H8 — On-page / Off-page / Links / Content / Traffic

**What is on-page SEO?** → `/en/blog/on-page-seo`  
On-page SEO is optimizing titles, headings, content, internal links, and media on a URL so it clearly matches search intent and earns clicks. It is the highest-control lever after technical health.

**What is off-page SEO?** → `/en/blog/off-page-seo`  
Off-page SEO is signals earned outside your site — mainly backlinks, brand mentions, and reputation. Strong off-page work supports pages that already deserve to rank on-page.

**How to write SEO content?** → `/en/blog/seo-content-guide`  
Pick one primary intent, answer it in the first screen, use clear headings for related questions, add proof (data, examples), and link to related services. Write for humans first; keywords guide structure, not stuffing.

**How to increase organic traffic?** → `/en/blog/how-to-increase-organic-traffic`  
Win more impressions by covering the right topics, improve CTR with better titles, fix indexation issues, and build internal links from strong pages. Compound with consistent publishing and relevant links.

**What is organic traffic?** → glossary + blog  
Organic traffic is visits from unpaid search results (and increasingly AI referrals that cite your pages). It excludes paid ads, email, and most social clicks.

**What is link building?** → `/en/blog/link-building-guide` + `/en/services/link-building`  
Link building is earning hyperlinks from other sites to yours. Quality and relevance matter more than volume — one editorial link from a trusted site beats dozens of spam directories.
