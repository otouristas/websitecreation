## What Drives the Cost of SEO

### 1. Competition in your niche

SEO for a law firm in a capital city costs more than SEO for a local trade in a smaller town: more content, more links, and more time to outrank established competitors. Dense markets need denser execution.

### 2. Your website's current state

A slow site with technical issues needs fixing first. If your site is dated, a [redesign with a safe SEO migration](/en/services/website-redesign) often pays off more than patching endlessly. If you do not have a site yet, start with [SEO-ready web design](/en/services/seo-web-design) — otherwise you pay twice.

### 3. Your goals

Local dominance in one city costs less than ranking nationally for competitive terms, which requires systematic content and link building. Online stores should also plan for [e-shop SEO](/en/services/eshop-seo).

### 4. Content and links

Articles, service pages, and quality backlinks are the fuel of SEO. The more you need, the larger the package. Technical SEO alone rarely moves the business without content that answers real searches.

## What a Serious SEO Package Should Include

- **Technical SEO**: speed, indexability, structured data (schema)
- **Keyword research** grounded in real Google Search Console data
- **On-page optimization** of service and location pages
- **Local SEO**: Google Business Profile, citations, reviews
- **Monthly reporting** with measurable progress (rankings, clicks, leads)
- Larger tiers: link building, GEO/AEO for AI engines, more content

If someone sells “full SEO” without explaining who writes the copy and which tools measure progress, ask before you sign.

## The Cheap-SEO Trap

€80–100/mo offers almost always mean automated reports, zero real work, or spam tactics that risk a Google penalty. Before you sign, ask:

1. What exactly happens each month?
2. How is progress measured, and what tools do I get access to?
3. Who writes the content, and to what standard?
4. Is there a contract lock-in?

## How to Think About SEO ROI

SEO is not a one-day ad buy. Estimate how many leads organic search already brings, your average profit per lead, and how many extra leads you need to cover the monthly retainer. If a lead is worth €80 net and the package is €299, you need roughly four extra leads per month to break even — often realistic in local markets within a quarter.

Demand a baseline before you start: rankings, Search Console clicks, form submits. Without a starting line, every report is a story. End partnerships that only report “position for one keyword” with no leads or revenue.

## What to Budget Alongside SEO

- Technical fixes if the crawl finds critical issues
- Extra content beyond the package (new service pages)
- Photography or creative for location pages
- A 3–6 month window before you judge results finally

If the site cannot support the work technically, SEO budget disappears into patches. In those cases, fund a [redesign](/en/services/website-redesign) or [SEO-ready build](/en/services/seo-web-design) first.

## How Agencies Usually Phase Work

Month one is diagnosis and technical cleanup. Months two and three expand on-page and local signals. From month four onward you should see compounding gains on the keywords you agreed — not a surprise list of vanity terms. Ask for that roadmap in writing before you pay the first invoice.

## FAQ

### How much does SEO cost for a small business?

For local visibility in one city, a €299/mo package is usually enough to see meaningful improvement within 3–4 months.

### Is SEO worth it, or should I spend on Google Ads?

They are different tools. Ads bring instant traffic while you pay; SEO builds durable organic flow that stays. See our full comparison: [SEO vs Google Ads](/en/blog/seo-vs-google-ads).

### How long until SEO pays off?

Low-competition local searches: 2–3 months. Competitive national keywords: 4–6+ months. SEO compounds — each month builds on the last.

### Is a #1 ranking guaranteed?

Anyone who “guarantees” #1 is not being honest — not even Google guarantees it. Ask for measurable goals and transparent reporting instead.

### What should a monthly report include?

Rankings for agreed keywords, organic clicks/impressions, leads or conversions where tracking exists, and a clear list of work completed — not a 40-page PDF of vanity charts.
