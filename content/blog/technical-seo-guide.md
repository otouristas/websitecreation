**Technical SEO** is the foundation under content and links: making your site easy for Google to crawl, index, and render — fast on mobile, with clean canonicals and structured data. If technical health is broken, publishing more blogs rarely helps.

For in-house ticket workflows, also see our [technical SEO for in-house teams](/en/blog/pillar-technical-seo-in-house) hub.

## Technical SEO checklist (priority order)

1. **Indexation & canonicals** — robots.txt, no accidental noindex, self-referencing canonicals
2. **Core Web Vitals** — LCP, INP, CLS on key templates ([speed optimization](/en/services/speed-optimization))
3. **Mobile crawlability** — usable tap targets, readable type
4. **Sitemaps & crawl paths** — XML sitemap, internal links to important URLs
5. **Structured data** — FAQ, LocalBusiness, Product, BreadcrumbList where accurate
6. **Hreflang / i18n** — correct EN/EL pairs for bilingual sites
7. **JS rendering** — critical content available without fragile client-only paths

Technical work does not replace relevance. It removes friction so [on-page SEO](/en/blog/on-page-seo) and [link building](/en/blog/link-building-guide) can work. Pair with the [What is SEO?](/en/blog/what-is-seo) pillar for the full picture.

## FAQ

### Is technical SEO hard?

Basics (HTTPS, sitemap, mobile, vitals) are learnable. Complex JS sites and migrations need experienced help.

### Does site speed affect rankings?

Yes as part of page experience — and it heavily affects conversion even when ranking impact is modest.
