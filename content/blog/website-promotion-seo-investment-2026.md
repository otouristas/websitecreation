## Why SEO is an investment, not a three-month expense

SEO is not a switch. Search engines need to discover or revisit your pages, process the changes, evaluate relevance and quality, and compare you with competitors. Meanwhile content, internal links and authority need time to accumulate.

That is why a serious SEO budget should be evaluated like any growth investment: **what capital goes in, which assets get created, which metrics move, and what is the business return?** A service page that ranks and brings enquiries every week is an asset. An ad that stopped is not. If you are new to the discipline, [what is SEO](/en/blog/what-is-seo) covers the fundamentals in ten minutes.

## Why SEO takes time

There is no legitimate way to promise that a new site will be first in two days. It can be indexed quickly. That is not the same as winning competitive rankings.

Three things take time: Google crawling and processing the changes, new and refreshed pages accumulating signals, and the team collecting enough Search Console data to tell a good hypothesis from a weak one. Six months is a practical operating window for foundations, iteration and measurement. It is not a guarantee that every site will hit a specific result by month six.

## What happens each month in a serious six-month SEO programme

| Month | Core work | What we expect to see |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Audit, Search Console and GA4 baseline, keyword-to-page map, technical fixes, indexation | A clean base, not necessarily a traffic spike |
| 2 | Money-page optimisation, titles and H1s, content gaps, schema, internal linking | New impressions and better query relevance |
| 3 | Content clusters, local and vertical pages, conversion improvements, authority work | More keywords within striking distance of page one |
| 4 | Refresh based on Search Console, cannibalisation fixes, links and mentions, GEO/AEO expansion | Better average positions and CTR in winning clusters |
| 5 | Scale winners, strengthen the internal graph, new commercial opportunities | More clicks and enquiries from mature pages |
| 6 | ROI review, prune or merge weak assets, expand profitable clusters | A clear picture for scaling, not an arbitrary "end of SEO" |

### Month 1: measure before you grow

Before we create dozens of new articles, we want a baseline. Search Console queries and pages, indexation status, GA4 organic sessions, enquiries and conversions, crawl errors, Core Web Vitals, the backlink profile and the competitors. Without a baseline, reporting becomes storytelling.

In parallel, the technical blockers get closed: pages that do not index, wrong canonicals, slow images, broken redirects. It is the least glamorous work and the most necessary.

### Month 2: optimise the pages that can bring money

Titles, headings, search intent, copy, entities, FAQs, schema, internal links and conversion paths. The priority is not "write a lot". It is to improve the URLs that can turn visibility into customers: the service page, the location page, the booking page. The [SEO content guide](/en/blog/seo-content-guide) shows what a page that both ranks and gets quoted looks like.

### Month 3: topical coverage and new opportunities

We create supporting content only where there is real demand or a business reason. For a hotel it may be destination, room and experience queries. For a rent-a-car, airport, port, location, fleet and use-case pages. For an e-shop, categories, subcategories, buying guides and product information.

### Month 4: Search Console starts deciding the next moves

Now we have real impressions and query patterns. We find URLs in positions 4 to 20, low CTR, two pages competing for the same query, new long-tail demand and content that needs a refresh. SEO moves from hypothesis to feedback loop. The [internal linking audit checklist](/en/blog/internal-linking-audit-checklist) is the tool we run at this point.

### Month 5: authority and scaling the winners

Pages showing traction get more internal support, updates, supporting assets and, where needed, editorial link acquisition and digital PR. We do not spread the budget evenly across every URL. We put it where the data says it works.

### Month 6: business review

We measure not only rankings but organic enquiries, bookings or revenue where attribution exists, assisted conversions, branded and non-branded growth, the winning clusters and cost per organic acquisition. Then we decide what to scale.

## What you should receive every month

A monthly SEO report worth its money has five parts. If one is missing, ask why.

| Part of the report | What it contains | Why it matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Change log | Every page, title, link and technical fix shipped that month | Proves work happened and lets you connect it to outcomes |
| Metric movement | Clicks, impressions, CTR, average position per cluster, enquiries | Shows the trend, not one position for one keyword |
| Findings | What Search Console and GA4 showed, what surprised us, what failed | Without findings there is no learning, only repetition |
| Next priorities | What happens next month and why that first | Lets you agree or change direction |
| Business link | How the metrics connect to bookings, enquiries or revenue | SEO is paid for customers, not for charts |

## Why e-commerce SEO costs more

An e-shop has a larger surface: categories, filters, products, pagination, structured data, out-of-stock logic, duplicate and near-duplicate URLs, internal linking, merchandising, feeds and often JavaScript complexity. "€500 SEO for everyone" ignores scope. See what changes in [e-shop SEO](/en/services/eshop-seo).

## Hotels, rent-a-car and local business: different playbooks

A [hotel](/en/solutions/hotels) competes for direct-booking intent, destination entities, room and experience pages, local signals and content that answers travel-planning questions. A [rent-a-car](/en/solutions/rent-a-car) operator competes across locations, airports, ports, vehicle classes and local demand. A B2B service company needs problem, solution and use-case architecture. A local business in one city needs [local SEO](/en/services/local-seo) and Google Business Profile first.

The strategic model is shared. The execution is not. There is no single SEO checklist that produces the same result everywhere.

## When an SEO retainer is poor value

When there are no deliverables, no baseline, no access to data, no business KPIs and no change log. When the report consists only of 50 keyword positions. When the agency cannot explain what changed this month and why. And when it promises rankings it does not control.

It is also poor value when the site cannot carry it technically. If the foundation is slow and has no architecture, the SEO budget is spent on patches. In those cases a [redesign](/en/services/website-redesign) or an SEO-ready build comes first, and promotion second.

There is no fixed deadline. Six months is a useful planning horizon for foundations, content, iteration and measurement. It is not a guarantee that a result arrives by month six.

### Can anyone guarantee page-one Google rankings?

Not credibly, for a competitive query by a fixed date. A page being indexed is not the same as it ranking. Anyone promising positions they do not control is selling something else.

### What should I receive from an SEO engagement every month?

A change log of what was done, movement in the core metrics, findings from Search Console and Analytics, the next priorities and a clear link to business objectives. A list of 50 keyword positions is not a report.

### Why does e-commerce SEO cost more?

Because the surface is far larger. Categories, filters, products, pagination, structured data, out-of-stock states, duplicate URLs and internal linking need more technical and editorial work every month.

### How is website promotion different from Google Ads?
