**Link building** is the practice of earning hyperlinks from other websites to yours. Search engines treat quality links as votes of trust. Done poorly, it risks penalties; done well, it compounds [off-page SEO](/en/blog/off-page-seo) and helps competitive pages climb. In 2026 the winning pattern is simple: publish pages worth linking to, then earn editorial placements from relevant sites - never mass spam.

The **backlink building strategies** below are the six we still use: linkable assets, digital PR, relevant guest contributions, broken-link replacements, partnerships, and unlinked-mention reclamation. Further down you will find a prospect sheet and outreach email examples you can copy. Before you pitch anyone, export who already links to the pages you want to beat with a [backlink checker](/en/blog/backlink-checker).

Before outreach, fix [on-page SEO](/en/blog/on-page-seo) and [internal linking](/en/blog/internal-linking-audit-checklist) so authority can flow to the URLs you care about. Then consider [link building services](/en/services/link-building) if you need disciplined outreach.

## Backlink Building Strategies That Still Work

Pick the strategy from the asset you actually have. A pitch with nothing to cite fails, however polite the email is.

| Strategy | Use it when | What you offer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Linkable asset | Writers in your niche need a source | Original data, a calculator, a map, a definitive page |
| Digital PR | You have news, research, or a quotable expert | A short brief and a person who will answer |
| Guest contribution | A real niche site's readers are your buyers | An original article the host would publish unsigned |
| Broken-link replacement | A resource page links to a dead URL | A better page on the same topic |
| Partnership | You already work with the organisation | A genuine mention, not a directory blast |
| Unlinked mention | Someone already named you | A polite ask to add the citation |

### 1. Linkable assets

Original data, calculators, maps, industry benchmarks, destination guides and definitive playbooks attract links because they help other writers. For Greek tourism and local brands, unique seasonal data and high-quality photography packages often outperform generic “ultimate guides.”

### 2. Digital PR and journalist outreach

Newsworthy angles - openings, awards, research, expert commentary - earn coverage on real publications. Pitch value, not “please link to us.” Pair with [SEO content](/en/blog/seo-content-guide) that journalists can cite.

### 3. Relevant guest contributions

Editorial guest posts on niche sites still work when the article is useful and the host is real. Spam farms and spun networks do not. Relevance beats Domain Authority vanity scores.

### 4. Broken-link and resource-page opportunities

Find resource pages in your niche with dead links; suggest your better asset as a replacement. Classic, still effective, low risk.

### 5. Partnerships and associations

Suppliers, chambers, tourism boards, event partners and membership directories can yield relevant links when the relationship is genuine - not a blast to 500 directories.

### 6. Unlinked mention reclamation

Search for brand mentions without links; politely ask for a citation. Fast wins when PR already exists.

## What to Avoid Completely

- Private blog networks (PBNs)
- Mass blog comment and forum spam
- Irrelevant paid link packages
- Exact-match anchor schemes
- Sudden unnatural spikes from low-quality domains

If a vendor cannot show you the placement URL before or after publishing, walk away. Glossary: [Backlink](/en/glossary?term=backlink) concepts still matter; the tactics have matured.

## Prospect template

Copy this sheet and keep one row per site. Fill the row before you write the email. If the "reason" cell is empty, you do not have an outreach yet.

| Field | What to write |
| :--- | :--- |
| Their page | URL you hope will link, and the sentence where a link would sit |
| Your page | The single URL you want cited |
| Fit | Topic overlap, in one sentence |
| Reason | Dead URL, missing source, unlinked mention, or a useful asset |
| Contact | Named editor or author, or the real contact page |
| Status | Not sent, sent, follow-up, placed, or declined |

Send one email per row. Do not paste the same pitch across a list of unrelated domains.

## Outreach email examples

These are templates. Replace every bracket after you have read their page. Ask for one URL. Follow up once, about a week later, then stop. Nothing here is a claim about reply rates.

### Broken-link replacement

> Subject: Dead link on [page title]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I was reading [article title]. The link to [what the dead page covered] in the [section name] no longer resolves.
>
> We published [your page title], which covers the same point, including [one concrete detail a reader would miss]. It may work as a replacement: [your URL]
>
> Either way, thank you for keeping the page up.
>
> [Your name]

### Unlinked mention

> Subject: [Publication] mentioned [Brand]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for mentioning [Brand] in [article title]. The line about [the specific point they made] is fair.
>
> If that page is still open for edits, a link to [your URL] would give readers the source. No problem if the piece is already closed.
>
> [Your name]

### Resource or guest contribution

> Subject: Source for [their page topic]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> [Their page title] is the page I send people who ask about [topic]. It does not yet cover [the specific gap: a checklist, a local example, a dataset].
>
> We put that together here: [your URL]. Use it if it earns a place. If a short original note is more useful, I can draft about 400 words for you to edit or reject.
>
> [Your name]

### The only follow-up

> Subject: Re: [original subject]
>
> Hi [Name], checking once in case my note last week was buried. I will leave it if the page is not taking new sources.
>
> [Your name]

The brackets are the strategy. They force you to name their page, their gap, and one URL of yours. A template without those three is a bulk pitch, and bulk pitches are the tactic in the "avoid" list above.

## Link Building Checklist Before You Spend

1. **Money pages convert** - do not promote a weak offer page.
2. **Technical crawl is clean** - [technical SEO](/en/blog/technical-seo-guide) and indexation OK.
3. **Internal links point equity** to the URLs you will promote.
4. **Competitor backlink sample** - who links to them, and why? Pull it with a [backlink checker](/en/blog/backlink-checker) and keep the export next to the prospect sheet.
5. **Asset list** - what can you offer that deserves a link?
6. **Prospect criteria** - topical fit, real traffic, editorial standards.
7. **Anchor policy** - mostly brand, URL and natural phrases.
8. **Reporting format** - URL, date, anchor, target page, notes.

## Seven Link Building Mistakes

1. **Buying links before fixing the site** - wasted spend.
2. **Chasing DA/DR only** - irrelevant high scores underperform niche editors.
3. **Identical commercial anchors** - manipulative patterns.
4. **No content worth linking to** - outreach without assets fails.
5. **Ignoring local/off-page reputation** - reviews and citations for local queries ([local SEO](/en/blog/local-seo-guide)).
6. **Guaranteed #1 promises** - ethical providers never guarantee positions.
7. **Stopping after ten links** - authority compounds; consistency wins.

## How Professional Link Building Services Should Run

A serious provider scopes **target pages**, competitor gaps and asset needs before sending a single email. You should receive: prospect criteria, sample pitches, monthly placement reports (live URLs), anchor mix notes and a clear “no PBN / no spam network” policy. If the pitch is only “we will get you 20 DA40 links,” treat it as a red flag.

Good campaigns sequence like this: audit → asset brief → prospecting → outreach → follow-up → reporting → iterate. Placements support money pages and topical hubs, not only the homepage. Anchors stay mostly branded and natural. Content and technical readiness are prerequisites - see [SEO audits](/en/services/seo-audits) and [content creation](/en/services/content-creation).

For Greek hospitality and local SMBs, prioritize publishers your buyers actually read: tourism media, city magazines, industry associations and niche blogs. A link from a relevant mid-authority site with real traffic beats a random “DA70” site your customers never visit. Track assisted conversions when possible; authority without leads is incomplete ROI.

Budget for patience. Even strong campaigns rarely flip competitive head terms in thirty days. Combine links with ongoing publishing so each placement lands on a page that keeps improving - that is how [organic traffic](/en/blog/how-to-increase-organic-traffic) compounds.

## FAQ

### What is link building?

Earning hyperlinks from other sites to yours. Quality and relevance beat volume.

### How many backlinks do I need?

Enough to compete with pages already ranking - measured by relevance and authority, not a fixed count.

### Are paid links safe?

Paying for pure ranking manipulation is against guidelines. Paying for PR, content and legitimate sponsorships with editorial control is a different category - demand transparency.

### How much does it cost?

Serious white-hat work often €400–€1,500+/mo or inside {{SEO_GROWTH}}–{{SEO_AUTHORITY}}+ retainers. Cheap bulk packages are usually spam.

### Do you guarantee rankings?

No. We guarantee process quality and reporting. Rankings depend on competition, content and algorithms.

### What are backlink building strategies?

Repeatable ways to earn editorial links: linkable assets, digital PR, relevant guest contributions, broken-link replacements, genuine partnerships, and unlinked-mention reclamation. Each one starts from a page worth citing.

### What should an outreach email include?

One specific page of theirs, one reason a link helps their readers, and one URL of yours. Templates for a broken-link replacement, an unlinked mention, and a resource pitch are above. Send one relevant email, follow up once, then stop.
