**Keyword research** finds the phrases your buyers type into Google so you can build pages that match intent — not guesswork. Head terms like “keyword research” itself are dominated by free tools; this guide shows the process, then points you to our [free keyword research tool](/en/tools/keyword-research).

## Step 1 — Collect seed topics

List products, services, problems, and locations you sell. Add queries you already impress for in Search Console.

## Step 2 — Expand ideas

Use autocomplete, People Also Ask, competitor pages, and a keyword tool. Capture **SEO keywords**, questions, and modifiers (near me, cost, best, vs).

## Step 3 — Label search intent

- **Informational** — learn (what is, how to)
- **Commercial** — compare (best, reviews, agency)
- **Transactional** — buy (services, hire, pricing)
- **Navigational** — find a brand

## Step 4 — Cluster and map

Group synonyms onto one page. Give each URL one primary phrase. Avoid thin duplicates for every minor variant.

## Step 5 — Prioritize

Balance volume, keyword difficulty, and revenue. New sites should attack commercial long-tails before national head terms. See our [EN SEO hub research](/en/blog/what-is-seo) for how topics connect.

Start with Search Console + our [keyword research clustering tool](/en/tools/keyword-research). Add paid databases when you need scale. Roundup: [best SEO tools 2026](/en/blog/best-seo-tools-2026).

## FAQ

### How many keywords should a page target?

One primary intent plus natural variants in headings and FAQs — not a stuffed list.

### How often should you refresh keyword research?

Quarterly for competitive niches; after every major product or location change.
