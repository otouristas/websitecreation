**SEO for hotels** in 2026 is no longer “rank for hotel + destination” and hope. Guests compare **OTAs**, Google Hotels, Maps and AI trip planners before they tap Book. Properties that win **direct bookings** own a fast site, clear room-intent pages, destination content and proof worth citing. This guide shows how Greek hospitality brands reduce Booking and Expedia dependency without disappearing from distribution.

## The Hotel SEO Stack That Converts

### Room-type and amenity pages

A generic homepage will not win “suite with private pool Santorini” or “family room Chania.” You need **unique URLs** per room type - own title, H1, photos, amenities and booking CTA. Do the same for high-intent amenities: infinity pool, sea view, pet-friendly, adults-only.

### Schema and technical foundations

Hotel / LodgingBusiness schema with check-in, geo, offers and room types helps Google and AI systems understand the property. Parallel requirements: Core Web Vitals in range, mobile-first booking UX, and hreflang EL/EN when you sell to both markets. Without technical hygiene, content underperforms - see [technical SEO](/en/blog/technical-seo-guide) and [website creation for hotels](/en/solutions/hotels/website-creation).

### Local and destination SEO

Most bookings start with local intent: “hotel near Heraklion airport,” “boutique hotel Nafplio.” Build landing pages for beaches, villages, airports and experiences - not copy-paste templates. [Local SEO](/en/blog/local-seo-guide) and a healthy Google Business Profile are mandatory for Maps and the Local Pack.

### GEO / AEO for AI trip planners

Travelers ask “best family hotel in Crete with pool” inside ChatGPT. You need visible FAQs, clear answers and proof (reviews, awards, distinctive amenities) that systems can cite. Pair classic SEO with [AI Visibility](/en/services/ai-visibility) and the [GEO vs SEO vs AEO](/en/blog/geo-vs-seo-vs-aeo) primer.

## 2026 On-Page and Content Checklist

1. **One primary intent per URL** - not every room type crammed into one thin page.
2. **Title + H1** with room type and destination, written naturally.
3. **Answer above the fold** - what the room is, who it suits, from-price, CTA.
4. **Compressed images + alt text** - multi-MB galleries destroy Core Web Vitals.
5. **Visible FAQs** for People Also Ask and AI Overviews.
6. **Internal links** from blog and destination pages to room + booking URLs.
7. **Reviews and social proof** above the fold on mobile.
8. **WhatsApp / phone / form** beside the booking engine.

For deeper page craft, read [on-page SEO](/en/blog/on-page-seo) and the [SEO content guide](/en/blog/seo-content-guide).

## Six Mistakes Greek Hotels Still Make

1. **Thin template pages** that match every competitor on the island - Google has no reason to prefer you.
2. **Unoptimized galleries** - 4MB images, no alt, no lazy load.
3. **OTA branding only** - you train guests (and Google) that OTAs own the query.
4. **Missing hreflang** on Greek/English sites - language mix-ups and cannibalization.
5. **Homepage as the only landing page** - nothing ranks for “honeymoon suite” or “rooms with jacuzzi.”
6. **Neglected Google Business Profile** - photos, posts, Q&A and review replies idle all season.

## A 90-Day Direct-Booking Sprint

**Days 1–30:** technical audit (speed, indexation, schema, hreflang), Google Business Profile cleanup, and prioritize room URLs using Search Console plus OTA performance data. Fix broken mobile CTAs and compress galleries that destroy Core Web Vitals.

**Days 31–60:** publish or rewrite 4–8 room/amenity pages, 2–3 destination landings, visible FAQs, and internal links from editorial content to booking URLs. Start weekly GBP posts and consistent review replies.

**Days 61–90:** measure CTR and direct-booking share, A/B titles, begin digital PR / local links, and tighten GEO answers for AI planners. Report organic clicks, positions and site bookings vs OTA - not sessions alone.

If the property also needs a new site, combine SEO with [hotel website creation](/en/solutions/hotels/website-creation) and a conversion-first booking UX. Rankings without a frictionless reserve path leave money on the table. Seasonality matters: start the sprint **before** peak (ideally autumn–winter for summer destinations) so content has time to index. City hotels see steadier demand; islands require year-over-year comparisons.

## FAQ

### What is SEO for hotels?

Optimizing the property site, local signals and content so guests **find and book directly** - cutting OTA commissions without vanishing from distribution channels.

### How long until results?

Local and branded queries often move in 2–3 months. Competitive destinations need 4–6+ months of consistent work. SEO compounds; each season builds on the last.

### Do I need GEO / AEO?

Yes if you want visibility in AI trip planners. It complements classic SEO with structured answers and citations - it does not replace it.

### How much does it cost?

Typically €299–€999 per month. Website packages from €899. Request a scoped plan via [Get Started](/en/get-started?project=hotel) with your OTA-dependency goal.

### Should I cut OTAs completely?

Rarely in phase one. Aim for **channel balance**: more direct bookings, better margin, OTAs as complementary reach.
